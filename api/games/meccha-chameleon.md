## What Is Meccha Chameleon?

Meccha Chameleon throws players into tense hide-and-seek firefights where nothing is quite what it seems. One team transforms into everyday objects scattered around the map, while the other team hunts them down with weapons ready. The twist? Props can rotate, zoom, and even taunt to throw hunters off track.

Every round lasts just a few minutes, but the pressure never lets up. Props must pick spots that look natural in their surroundings. Hunters need to manage their limited health pool carefully, since wrong guesses waste precious shots. This push-and-pull between hiding and hunting gives Meccha Chameleon its own rhythm compared to standard shooters.

## How to Play

### Two Roles, Two Skill Sets

When you spawn as a prop, you have 30 seconds to find a hiding spot and press E to transform into an object. Smaller items like cups or books blend better than oversized furniture. Try to use Q to taunt and mislead hunters, or stay completely silent and hope they walk right past you.

As a hunter, your job is to eliminate every prop before the timer hits zero. Switch between three weapons using numpad keys, aim with your mouse, and fire at anything that looks out of place. Every missed shot chips away at your health, so random spraying will punish careless play.

### Tips for Both Sides

Props could use corners with visual clutter for better cover than empty walls. Tiny objects draw less attention than large ones. If a hunter gets close, a well-timed taunt from another room could pull them away.

Hunters must treat strange placement as their biggest clue. A chair floating slightly off the ground or a book facing the wrong direction often means a player is hiding there. Try to listen for taunt audio cues after the 30-second setup phase ends.

## Why It Works

Meccha Chameleon keeps matches short and social. This is a 5v5 format, meaning you are never alone in your role, and the prop mechanics reward creativity over raw aim. Hunters need game sense and teamwork to sweep rooms efficiently. Neither side feels like an afterthought, which keeps lobbies full and rounds unpredictable.
